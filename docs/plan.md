# Told by Mouth: plan

Written Oct 25, 2026 by Byung and Claude, copied into this repo on Oct 29, 2026. Original doc: https://claude.ai/artifact/XS9wxEurV6oWhyq19vSyo6

> The app was called "mouth to mouth to mouth" (M3) when this was written. It is now **Told by Mouth** (renamed Oct 29, 2026, because English speakers hear CPR in "mouth to mouth"; the new name plays on 구전동화, folk tales passed on by word of mouth). Older wording below is kept as written.

## TL;DR

The app becomes a **real-world restaurant collection and review app**. You collect a restaurant by photographing its food there, trade restaurants with people you meet, and pass real-name reviews **only face to face**, phone to phone. The name says it: a mouth eats, a mouth tells, another mouth eats.

- **Fake reviews are stopped three ways:** a verified real name, proof you were there (an in-app photo taken at the restaurant's location), and a digital signature on every review so nobody can forge or edit it as it passes from phone to phone.
- **Screens:** fully native, **SwiftUI** on iPhone and **Jetpack Compose** on Android, for the best feel, animations and design (Byung's choice).
- **Shared Rust core:** rules, reviews, signatures, trading and Bluetooth logic live once in Rust. **UniFFI** generates the Swift and Kotlin bindings, so both apps call the same Rust code.
- **Reuse:** the Rust workspace, the trade handshake and the nearby layer carry over. Creatures become restaurants. The Bevy screens and desktop window are retired.
- **Needs a small server** for identity checks only. Reviews themselves still travel only in person.

## How the app works

There are three things a player does. Only the first happens alone.

| Action | Where | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Collect** a restaurant | At the restaurant | Take a photo of the food with the in-app camera (no gallery uploads). The app reads your location, lists restaurants within about 50 m from a map service, and you pick yours. You get a restaurant card with the photo, date and place. |
| **Trade** restaurants | Face to face, over Bluetooth | Same handshake we already built: each picks a card, both confirm, cards swap. A traded card says who collected it, but it never counts as your own visit. |
| **Share reviews** | Face to face, over Bluetooth | You can only review a restaurant you collected yourself. When two people meet, their phones swap reviews. Each review shows the real name of the person who ate there and who told it to you. |

A review is never posted to a public website. You only see reviews from people you met, or from people they met, so every review reaches you through a chain of real faces.

## Stopping fake reviews

A fake review needs a fake person, a fake visit, or a forged text. Each layer blocks one of them.

1. **Real name, checked once.** At sign-up the user passes an identity check from a verification provider (which one depends on your country; see open questions). Our small server then issues a certificate that says "this public key belongs to this real name". The private key stays locked in the phone's secure chip (Secure Enclave on iPhone, Keystore on Android).
2. **Proof of visit.** A review can only be written for a restaurant you collected yourself. The collect photo must come from the in-app camera, with the location within about 50 m of the restaurant, and the phone signs that record at the moment of capture.
3. **Signed reviews.** Every review is signed with the reviewer's key. Any phone can check, even offline, that the named person wrote it and that no word changed since.
4. **The mouth-to-mouth chain.** When someone passes your review on, their phone adds its own signature: "told by". So you always see who ate there and who told you, and a review can't be passed further than a set number of mouths.

What this does not stop: a real, verified person who writes a dishonest review, or a rooted phone faking its location. Apple App Attest and Google Play Integrity make the second much harder. Real names also make the reviewer accountable, which is the point, but it brings legal and privacy duties, covered under open questions.

## UI technology

**Decided: native screens with a shared Rust core.** Byung chose this for native feel, animations and design. Each platform gets its own screens in its own toolkit, and both call one Rust library for everything that isn't drawing.

| Layer | iPhone | Android |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screens and animation | SwiftUI | Jetpack Compose |
| Camera, location, Bluetooth | Swift (AVFoundation, Core Location, CoreBluetooth) | Kotlin (CameraX, location services, Android BLE) |
| Bridge to Rust | Swift bindings generated by UniFFI | Kotlin bindings generated by UniFFI |
| Rules, reviews, signatures, trading | Shared Rust core | Shared Rust core |
| Build | Xcode, on your Mac | Android Studio (Gradle) |

Options considered and set aside: Tauri + Leptos and Dioxus (one set of screens, but drawn by a web engine), and Bevy (a game engine; poor at typing, lists and photos).

The cost is writing each screen twice, once in Swift and once in Kotlin. The rule that keeps that manageable: screens only draw and react; every decision (can this be reviewed, is this signature valid, did the trade finish) lives in Rust, so the two apps can't disagree.

## Architecture

```
 iPhone app (SwiftUI)            Android app (Jetpack Compose)
 camera, location, BLE (Swift)   camera, location, BLE (Kotlin)
            \                          /
             \---- UniFFI bindings ---/
                        |
              shared Rust core (rules, restaurant cards,
              reviews, signatures, told-by chain, trading)
                        |
     online, rarely:  map lookup (when collecting)
                      identity server (one-time real-name check,
                      issues certificates, stores no reviews)
```

Screens are native on each phone and share one Rust core. The only online parts are the map lookup when collecting and the one-time name check; reviews and trades never leave the phones except face to face.

## Milestones

Each milestone ends with something you can run. I can build and test milestone 1 myself; the iPhone app needs your Mac to build, and Bluetooth needs real phones.

1. **New core.** Rename the project, turn creatures into restaurant cards, add reviews with signatures and the told-by chain, and the UniFFI bridge. Fully tested with simulated players, no screens.
2. **iPhone app.** SwiftUI screens for collect, my restaurants and write review, with the real camera and location. Runs in the iOS Simulator on your Mac.
3. **Android app.** The same screens in Jetpack Compose, in the Android emulator.
4. **Meeting over Bluetooth.** Swift and Kotlin Bluetooth code; an iPhone and an Android phone trade cards and swap reviews. Needs two real phones, since simulators have no Bluetooth.
5. **Real names.** The identity server, a verification provider, and certificates on every review.
6. **Hardening.** App Attest and Play Integrity checks against faked locations.

## Open questions for Byung

- **Which country first?** It decides the map service (Kakao or Naver in Korea, Google or Apple elsewhere) and the identity check (phone-based PASS check in Korea, ID document scan elsewhere).
- **Screens: decided.** Native SwiftUI and Compose screens with a shared Rust core.
- **Trading:** does a trade swap cards (you lose yours), or give each player a copy?
- **How far does a review travel?** Only to people the reviewer meets (eat, tell, eat), or onward through several people?
- **Taking a review back:** once shared in person, a review can't be deleted from other phones. A "withdrawn" notice could spread the same way. Is that enough?
- **Legal check:** real-name reviews carry defamation and privacy risk. In Korea even a true statement that harms a business's reputation can be a crime. A lawyer should look at this before launch.
- **Project name:** decided Oct 29, 2026: **Told by Mouth**. Repo: `brigh2ha/told-by-mouth`.
